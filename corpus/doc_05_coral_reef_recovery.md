# Coral Bleaching and the Limits of Biological Recovery

Coral reefs bleach when heat-stressed polyps expel the symbiotic algae that
give them both color and most of their energy, leaving a white skeleton that
is not yet dead but is starving. Reefs have recovered from bleaching events
for millions of years, individual colonies can regain their symbionts within
weeks if the water cools in time, and reef structure itself can regenerate
over subsequent decades if given enough time between stress events. What has
changed is the interval: recovery that once had centuries between major
disturbances now has to happen between events a few years apart, as marine
heat waves become more frequent. Resilience is not a fixed property of a
system; it is a race between the system's recovery time and the frequency of
the failures it has to recover from. A system that can recover from any
single failure can still be destroyed if failures arrive faster than
recovery completes.
