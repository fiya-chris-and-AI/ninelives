# Triple-Redundant Hydraulics in Commercial Aircraft

A wide-body airliner like the Boeing 777 carries three fully independent
hydraulic systems, each capable on its own of moving every critical flight
control surface, so that no single pump, line rupture, or engine failure can
leave the pilot without control authority. The three systems are physically
routed through different parts of the airframe specifically so that one
piece of shrapnel from an uncontained engine failure cannot sever all three
at once, a lesson learned directly from United Airlines Flight 232 in 1989,
where the simultaneous loss of all three hydraulic systems on a DC-10, a
design with far less physical separation, left the crew flying using only
engine thrust to steer. Redundancy that shares a single point of physical
failure is not really redundancy; the systems have to fail independently of
each other for the guarantee to hold.
