# Redundant Path Design in the Interstate Highway System

The U.S. Interstate Highway System was deliberately over-built with parallel
routes between major cities so that no single bridge failure or closure could
isolate a region. When the I-35W bridge in Minneapolis collapsed in 2007,
traffic rerouted within hours onto adjacent corridors that had been planned
decades earlier with exactly this kind of failure in mind. The system's
resilience was not a side effect of scale; it was a design decision to treat
every load-bearing link as eventually replaceable, and to make sure the
network's function never depended on any one of them staying up forever.
Engineers call this N+1 redundancy: build enough spare capacity that losing
any single component still leaves the whole system serviceable, even if
individual routes must absorb more traffic than they were sized for.
